ReXell · how entry works

A face becomes a number.
The number opens a door.

There is no ticket to show at the gate. At signup a face is measured into 128 numbers; at the door another 128 numbers are measured and compared. Nothing in between is ever a photograph — and the two measurements are never equal, which is why the whole system is built on similarity and never on a match.

Phase one · signing up

The face becomes a number

Four steps, on the phone of the person signing up. Because that phone belongs to them and not to us, nothing it claims is believed — it submits what it saw and the server reaches the verdict.

  1. 1
    The server picks a movement the phone cannot predict

    One of turn_left, turn_right, nod, blink, with a single-use nonce and a sixty-second life. A printed photograph cannot turn its head, and a video recorded in advance cannot know which way it will be asked to.

  2. 2
    The camera watches, and records what it saw

    About a dozen frames over two seconds. Each one carries its head pose, its eye openness, and its own descriptor — evidence, not a verdict. The boolean this replaced was set by the very code we are defending against.

    one frame of the submitted capture
    { at: 1260, yaw: 0.312, pitch: -0.04, eyeOpen: 0.29,
  vector: [-0.0307, 0.0918, -0.0351, …] }
  3. 3
    Detect, align on 68 landmarks, read 128 numbers

    A convolutional network reads the aligned crop. Its raw output is then centred — the average face subtracted — and normalised to unit length. Without the centring every face points into a narrow cone and two strangers already score 0.83; with it, the usable gap widens roughly tenfold.

  4. 4
    The vault judges the evidence, then seals the template

    It re-derives the verdict from the frames: was it the movement asked for, did it start from a face pointed at the lens, is every frame the same person, is any frame a copy of another. Only then is one vector sealed — 512 bytes under AES-256-GCM, with a per-row key that is itself wrapped. The other frames are read and dropped.

    what is on disk, in a database no ticketing code can reach
    template_ref   tpl_43e3a9d0649b47fabafe
model_version  prototype-cosine-128-v1
ciphertext     512 bytes   iv 12   tag 16   wrapped_key 32
Phase two · walking in

The number becomes a decision

Here the device belongs to the venue, and the attacker is whoever is standing in front of it. That inversion is what lets the lane pick its own challenge and decide for itself — which it must, because a lane that needs a server is a lane that stops when the venue's wifi does.

  1. 5
    The lane gets a sealed blob, days early if you like

    Every ticket-holder's template for one event, encrypted for one scanner. The event, the scanner, the sequence and the expiry are authenticated alongside it, so a blob relabelled for another lane fails to open rather than opening into the wrong event.

  2. 6
    Two hours before doors, the key is released

    Separately, and not a moment earlier. A scanner stolen the week before carries a blob and nothing that opens it. Only now does the lane decode the manifest into a gallery of 128-number templates it can compare against, entirely offline.

    decoded, in the lane's memory
    ticket tkt_f312c5b8…  identity idn_135f9e1b…  revoked false
template Float32Array(128)  ‖v‖ = 1.0000
  3. 7
    Somebody walks up, and is asked to move

    The lane picks a movement from the device's own CSPRNG and watches until it sees it — returning the moment it is satisfied rather than running a fixed clock. It accepts far less movement than signup does: a glance, not a performance, because a lane that makes four thousand people perform is a lane with a queue around the block.

  4. 8
    One number against every number in the gallery

    Cosine similarity — with unit vectors, simply a dot product — against all of them, and the best score wins. Twelve thousand comparisons take about eight milliseconds, which is the entire reason the manifest is scoped to one event rather than the whole platform.

    measured at the door, on the live deployment
    the ticket-holder, a photograph never seen before   0.8205
a stranger with no ticket                             0.4593
a printed photo of the ticket-holder                  0.8166  ← liveness failed
  5. 9
    The ladder, in a fixed order

    Match first, then liveness, then the policy rungs. The order is a disclosure decision: telling a stranger their liveness failed would tell them the face was recognised, and that is a fact about somebody else.

  6. 10
    The lane signs what it decided, and cannot take it back

    Every decision — including the refusals — is signed with a key that never leaves the device, queued, and uploaded when the network returns. The liveness verdict is inside the signed bytes, and a lane configured without the check signs the word none, so "never checked" and "checked and satisfied" can never be confused afterwards.

    the signed record
    rexell-attestation-v2 │ scn_4f2a │ Lane A │ tkt_f312c5b8…
1789140919055 │ admit │ MATCHED │ 0.994800 │ seq 3 │ online
turn_right:pass:5   ← the liveness line, signed like everything else

Where the line sits, and why there

Measured on 17 descriptors from real photographs of five people, not chosen. Two pictures of one person scored 0.664 at worst; two different people scored 0.543 at best. Every threshold lives in that gap.

What the door can say

Nine answers, and only three of them are a refusal. Nothing about a poor score is ever a denial — a false reject that sends somebody home turns a 1.5% error rate into 1.5% of the crowd losing an evening they paid for.

MATCHED

Recognised, alive, and the ticket is good for this door at this hour. Welcome in.

REENTRY

Already admitted, and this event allows coming back. Welcome back.

NO_MATCH

Nobody in this gallery scores above 0.50. Try once more, then the desk.

LOW_CONFIDENCE

Between 0.50 and 0.60. Look straight at the camera and try again.

LIVENESS_FAILED

The right face, and the lane could not satisfy itself anybody was there. From a camera, a photograph and a person in bad light are the same thing — only a human can tell them apart.

CREDENTIAL_REVOKED

Resold or cancelled. It belongs to somebody else now, and that reached this lane within thirty seconds of the sale.

ALREADY_ADMITTED

This ticket has been used to enter, and this event does not allow re-entry.

WRONG_GATE

A valid ticket at the wrong entrance. Direct them to the right one.

TOO_EARLY / TOO_LATE

Outside this ticket's admission window.

What this does not do

  • It stops paper, not screens. Neither check can tell a video played on a phone held up to the lens from a face, and neither stops a mask. This is challenge-response, not certified presentation-attack detection.
  • At signup, a modified client can lie. Every pose is measured in a browser we do not own. The evidence rules raise the cost of the easy attack from "print a photograph" to "write code"; they do not close it. At the lane the device is ours, so that gap does not exist there.
  • The thresholds come from five people. Enough to show the two distributions separate cleanly. It says nothing about the highest impostor score across a gallery of twelve thousand, which is the number an event actually depends on.
  • Liveness costs lanes. Five for an hour of ingress at 12,000 capacity, where one would do without it. That is a decision to take before the night, not during it.
