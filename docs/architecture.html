System Architecture / ReXell Technologies / v1.0 · 09 Sep 2026

Two identities, one gate, no transferable ticket.

ReXell replaces the ticket-as-a-document with a ticket-as-a-binding: an on-chain entitlement fused to a biometric credential, governed by resale rules the organizer writes themselves. This document specifies how that is built, what it must never do, and how it degrades when things break.

01Constraints

Constraints that shape everything

Architecture is mostly the residue of hard constraints. These are the eight that eliminated the obvious designs.

ConstraintWhy it bindsArchitectural consequence
Gate throughputA 12,000-capacity show clears its queue in roughly 90 minutes across 10–14 lanes: 1,200–2,500 scans per lane-hour.The verification decision must be local to the scanner. No network call, no chain read, no cloud match on the critical path.
Venue connectivityStadium and festival-ground cellular collapses under crowd load at exactly the moment the gate needs it.Offline-first scanner holding a pre-synced encrypted event manifest, with delta-based revocation sync.
Biometrics are irreversibleA leaked face template cannot be rotated like a password. It is that person, for life.Templates never leave a dedicated vault, and never touch application databases, logs, backups, or the chain.
Templates are non-deterministicTwo captures of the same face produce different vectors. The comparison is similarity, never equality.You cannot hash a face and compare hashes. This single fact kills the most common web3-ticketing design — see §03.
No PII on chainPublic ledgers are permanent. Erasure rights are not optional in the launch markets.On-chain state holds entitlements, policy and settlement proofs only. Identity sits off-chain behind a pseudonymous identityId.
Onsale is a thundering herdA hot onsale is 50–500× baseline traffic inside 60 seconds — and it is exactly where the bots are.Edge waiting room with fair queueing and signed admission tokens; inventory held in Redis, not by row locks in Postgres.
Fans without biometrics must still get inRefusal, disability, injury, religious objection, a dead phone, a minor without consent capacity.A documented, audited non-biometric path is a first-class feature, not an escape hatch. See §12.
Crypto must be invisibleFans pay in local currency and will not manage keys or gas, and virtual-asset tax treatment is punitive in the launch market.Account abstraction with a sponsored paymaster, custodial-by-default smart accounts, and fiat rails for every rupee that moves.
02System map

The system map

Five layers and one hard boundary. Everything above the boundary is hostile territory — a fan's phone, a scanner in a muddy field, a bot farm's headless browser. Everything below it is ours, and the biometric vault is a further enclave inside that.

2.1Why these service boundaries

The split follows blast radius, not org chart. Biometric Service is isolated because it is the one component whose compromise is unrecoverable. Ordering and Payments are separate because ordering must survive a payment-provider outage by holding inventory. Entry & Access is separate because it has to keep working when everything else is down. Token Service is the only writer to the chain, so every on-chain mutation has exactly one audited code path.

03Identity

Identity: real-world × digital

The product thesis is that fusing a real-world identity to a digital one defeats both the scalper and the counterfeiter. The engineering question is how you fuse them without creating a permanent, unrevocable biometric identifier on a public ledger. There is a wrong answer that looks right, and it is worth naming.

3.1The three identifiers

IdentifierLives whereWhat it is, and what it deliberately is not
personIdIdentity service, Postgres, encrypted at column levelThe legal person: name, contact, government-ID reference for age-gated events. Never leaves the identity service; other services receive only identityId.
identityIdEverywhere, including on chainA random opaque 128-bit handle. Carries no information. This is the pseudonym that lets the rest of the system, and the ledger, refer to a person without holding one.
templateRefBiometric vault onlyPointer to the sealed face template plus its model version. Never returned by any API. Rotating the model means re-enrolling, which is why the model version is stored alongside.
 A compromise of the main application database yields pseudonyms and ticket entitlements, not faces and not names.

3.2Enrolment

  1. Consent first. A separate, unbundled biometric consent screen naming purpose, retention period and the non-biometric alternative. Consent is versioned and stored as an immutable record; withdrawal is a supported operation, not a support ticket.
  2. Capture and liveness. Passive liveness on the frame stream plus one active challenge, run on device. This is the layer that stops a printed photo, a replayed video and a deepfake feed injected through a virtual camera.
  3. Embedding on device. The raw frames never leave the phone and are destroyed after extraction. What leaves is a template, encrypted to the vault's public key.
  4. Dedupe. A 1:N search within a bounded scope catches one person enrolling as five accounts — the account-farming primitive that every scalper relies on. Match above threshold links the accounts and flags them rather than silently blocking, because twins and siblings exist.
  5. Bind. Token Service writes identityId → smartAccount to AccessRegistry. From this point the fan's tickets are addressable without any reference to a person.

Design note — the twin problem

Face recognition cannot reliably separate identical twins, and 1:N dedupe at scale will produce false links. Neither is a reason to abandon the approach; both are reasons the system must never treat a biometric match as proof on its own. Dedupe flags feed a review queue; gate denial always has an operator override with an audit record. A biometric that cannot be appealed is a liability, not a feature.

04Token

The ticket as a token

A ReXell ticket is an ERC-721 token whose transferability is a property of the event, set by the organizer, and enforced in the contract rather than in the app. Two modes, one contract.

Mode A
BoundTransfer reverts. Ticket dies with its owner's entitlement.
Mode B
CappedTransfer allowed only through ResaleController.
Never
OpenNo unrestricted transfer path exists in either mode.

Mode A is soulbound in the practical sense: _beforeTokenTransfer reverts for any caller. Mode B keeps transfer permissioned to a single controller contract, which is where price caps, windows and commission splits are enforced. Crucially, there is no third mode. The token is never freely transferable, so it can never appear on a general NFT marketplace, which is how most “NFT ticketing” attempts leak straight back into unrestricted scalping.

4.1Contract surface

ContractHoldsKey entry points
EventFactoryDeployment registry, organizer rolescreateEvent(policyHash, tiers, capacity) deploys a per-event TicketNFT clone and emits the immutable policy hash.
TicketNFTToken ownership, tier, seat, mode, redemption statemintTo(identityId, tier), redeem(tokenId, attestation), revoke(tokenId, reason)
ResaleControllerListings, price caps, windows, cooldownslist(tokenId, priceMinor), buy(tokenId, buyerIdentityId) — reverts on cap breach, closed window, or an unverified buyer.
RoyaltySplitterBasis-point split table per eventdistribute(saleId) pays organizer, rights-holder, platform and seller in one transaction. Splits are set at event creation and immutable for listed inventory.
AccessRegistryidentityId → smartAccount, revocationsbind, rebind (recovery after a lost device), revokeCredential
 All five are upgradeable behind a proxy with a timelock plus multisig, except TicketNFT clones for live events, which are frozen once the first ticket sells.

Design note — chain of record, fiat of settlement

Money does not move on chain. Fans pay in local currency through a licensed payment aggregator; the contract records the entitlement and computes the split, and the splitter's emitted event is the instruction that the off-chain payout ledger reconciles against. This keeps the auditability benefit — every commission is independently verifiable — while avoiding virtual-asset tax treatment, stablecoin licensing, and the requirement that a concertgoer hold crypto.

The tradeoff is honest and should be stated to any technical buyer: settlement is trust-minimised, not trustless. On-chain stablecoin settlement remains available as an opt-in for organizers in jurisdictions where it is clean.

05Purchase

Purchase and issuance

Seven hops, and the notable thing about them is the order: the bot check happens before inventory is touched, and the identity check happens before money is taken. Reversing either turns a scalper attempt into a refund queue.

Mint is deferred and batched. A hot onsale cannot wait on block confirmation, so Ordering issues the entitlement in Postgres immediately, the fan sees a valid ticket at once, and Token Service mints asynchronously in batches with the database row as the source of truth until the transaction confirms. If the chain is unavailable for an hour, tickets still sell and the gate still works — the ledger simply catches up.

06The gate

The gate: an 800 ms budget

The gate is the product. Everything else can be a little slow; this cannot. The target is a p95 of 800 ms from face-in-frame to green light, and the only way to hit it at a festival is to make the decision without leaving the device.

6.1The event manifest

Each scanner holds an encrypted, event-scoped manifest containing one entry per admitted credential: the sealed template, identityId, tier, seat, and entry rules. It is a bounded set — twelve thousand faces, not twelve million — which is exactly why an on-device 1:N search is tractable at 45 ms.

  • Scoped. One manifest per event, per gate group. A scanner stolen from a stadium yields the templates of that night's ticket-holders and nothing else.
  • Time-boxed. Manifest keys are released to the device on a schedule tied to doors-open, and the whole manifest self-destructs on a TTL after the event ends. Verified deletion is reported back and audited.
  • Delta-synced. Every resale, revocation and late sale becomes a signed delta. Deltas apply in order and carry a sequence number, so a scanner that has been offline for an hour knows exactly how stale it is and can display that to the operator.
  • Attested. Each admission produces a signed attestation queued locally and uploaded when connectivity returns. Double-entry is detected on reconciliation, not prevented at the gate — which is the right trade, because the alternative is a gate that stops working when the network does.

The honest limitation

Because scanners are allowed to make decisions offline, two lanes can each admit the same credential during a network partition. This is bounded — deltas converge in seconds when connectivity is normal, and offline windows are logged — and it is a deliberate choice: an offline-tolerant gate with a rare, detectable double-entry is strictly better than an online-only gate that halts a queue of nine thousand people. The alternative design, hard-locking each credential to a single lane, is available for high-security events where throughput is less important than certainty.

07Resale

Programmable resale

The organizer-facing promise is a switch and three dials: resale on or off, a price ceiling, a window, and a commission. Those four settings compile down to conditions in ResaleController, so the policy is enforced by the token rather than by the marketplace's good behaviour.

7.1The organizer's dials

SettingRangeWhat it stops, and the failure it introduces
Resale offBooleanEliminates scalping outright. Introduces genuine hardship — illness, travel, a changed date — so pair it with a refund-to-face-value returns queue that resells at the original price. An organizer who turns resale off without a returns queue will generate the worst support load in the product.
Price ceiling100–150% of faceRemoves the profit motive from bulk buying. Pushes determined resale off-platform, which is why the ceiling matters less than the identity binding: an off-platform buyer gets a ticket their face cannot open.
Resale windowOpens T−n days, shuts T−h hoursStops both day-of price gouging and last-minute credential churn the scanners cannot sync. A window that shuts too early strands genuine sellers.
Cooldown0–72 hours after purchaseDefeats the buy-and-flip-instantly pattern that automated resale bots rely on.
Per-identity cap1–10 tickets, 1–3 resalesCaps the value of any single farmed account. Enforced against identityId, which is why biometric dedupe at enrolment is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
Commission0–15%, split any wayTurns the secondary market from revenue the organizer never sees into revenue they set the terms of. This is the commercial hook.
08Bots

Bot and scalper defense

The AI layer has one job: decide, in under 50 milliseconds, whether the entity requesting a ticket is a person. It does this with two models on two timescales — a fast scorer inline in the request path, and a slow graph model that finds the farm rather than the bot.

What this layer cannot do

A determined operator paying real humans in a low-wage market to buy tickets on real devices with real cards will pass every behavioural check, because they are not a bot. Behavioural detection raises the cost of scalping; it does not end it. The thing that actually ends it is the identity binding — a human-farmed ticket is still a ticket whose face does not match the person at the gate. The AI layer's real job is to keep the honest fan's queue clean and to make bulk acquisition expensive, not to be the last line of defense.

09Screenshots

“No screenshots”, honestly

The requirement is that a fan cannot screenshot a ticket and sell it online. The technical controls below help, and none of them is sufficient — a phone pointed at another phone's screen defeats every one. The architecture solves it a different way, by removing the artefact.

ControlWhat it genuinely stopsWhat it does not
FLAG_SECURE on AndroidOS screenshots, screen recording, and the ticket appearing in the app switcher or a screen share.A second camera. Rooted devices with modified frameworks.
iOS capture detectionNothing, preventatively — iOS gives notification, not prevention. Useful as a signal: a capture event raises the account's risk score.The capture itself. Treat it as telemetry, never as a control.
Rotating display codeA stale image being useful. Codes are derived from a device-held secret and rotate every 15 seconds, so a screenshot expires before it can be listed for sale.A live screen-share at the moment of entry.
the actual answerThere is nothing to screenshot. The credential is the fan's face plus a server-side entitlement. There is no barcode, no QR code, no PDF, and no image that constitutes a ticket. A screenshot of the ReXell app is a screenshot of a receipt.
 The rotating code exists only for the non-biometric fallback path, where a visual credential is unavoidable.

12Degradation

Degradation ladder

Every step down is a deliberate, pre-authorised behaviour with its own audit trail. The operator at the gate is told which rung they are on.

RungWhat has failedSystem behaviourCost accepted
0NothingFace scan, local match, green light.None.
1Venue connectivityScanner runs fully offline against its manifest; attestations queue.Deltas from the last few minutes are missing. Rare double-entry, detected on reconciliation.
2Biometric match fails twiceFan is routed to a resolution lane: app-held rotating code plus a liveness re-check on a staffed device.60–90 seconds per case. Sized for 1.5% of attendees.
3Fan cannot or will not use biometricsPre-registered non-biometric path: government ID checked against the order name by a trained operator.Slower, human-dependent. Must be offered without penalty or the product is discriminatory.
4Scanner hardware downAdjacent lane absorbs traffic; spare devices carry the same manifest, keyed on arrival.Throughput drops by one lane. Spares are a line item, not an optional extra.
5Manifest cannot be provisioned at allBreak-glass mode: signed offline ticket list, ID-based admission, biometrics suspended for the event. Requires two-person authorisation and produces an incident report.The event's fraud guarantee is void. This must be rare enough to be newsworthy internally.

14Privacy

Privacy and compliance

Biometrics move this product from “handle data carefully” to “processing a special category of personal data under multiple regimes at once.” The architecture is built so that compliance is a property of the design rather than a policy document.

14.1Retention schedule

DataRetainedThen
Raw face framesnever persistedDestroyed in-process after template extraction. Never written to disk, never logged.
Face templateenrolment + 12 monthsDeleted on inactivity, on withdrawal of consent, or immediately on request. Deletion is verified and certificated.
Event manifest on scannerdoors-open to +6 hCryptographic erasure on TTL, confirmed back to the server and audited per device.
Entry attestations90 days full, then aggregateMatch scores stripped; counts retained for capacity reporting.
Risk signals90 daysAggregated into account-level features; raw behavioural rows dropped.
Order and payment records7 yearsStatutory financial retention. Contains no biometric data, so it survives an erasure request lawfully.
On-chain recordspermanentPseudonymous by construction: identityId, token, policy, split. Erasure of the off-chain mapping renders them unlinkable, which is what makes permanence compatible with erasure rights.

14.2Regimes the design answers to

  • India DPDP Act, 2023 — itemised notice, purpose limitation, consent withdrawal as a first-class operation, breach notification, and a named data protection officer. The consent record model in §10 exists to satisfy this.
  • GDPR Article 9 — biometric data for unique identification requires explicit consent and a completed DPIA. Consent must be unbundled from the ticket purchase, which is why the enrolment flow has its own screen and its own record.
  • Illinois BIPA and comparable US state law — written release, a published retention schedule, and a prohibition on profiting from biometric data. The litigation exposure here is material and disproportionate; the recommendation is to geofence biometric enrolment out of those states and operate the non-biometric path there until counsel advises otherwise.
  • Accessibility — WCAG 2.2 AA across fan and organizer surfaces, and the non-biometric entry path treated as an equal route rather than an exception. A gate that works only for some bodies is not a gate.
  • Payments — card data never touches ReXell infrastructure; a licensed aggregator holds the merchant relationship and ReXell scopes to SAQ-A.

The design decision that makes all of this tractable

Because no biometric or personal data is ever written on chain, a deletion request is satisfiable in full: delete the template from the vault, tombstone the person record, and the permanent ledger is left holding an opaque identifier that maps to nobody. Any design that anchors biometric data on chain forfeits this permanently, on the first day, for every user.

17Open

Open decisions

Named, so they are decided rather than defaulted into.

  1. Custodial or non-custodial smart accounts at launch. Custodial gives a recoverable, gas-free experience for a mainstream fan; non-custodial is the stronger claim to a web3 buyer. Recommendation: custodial by default with an export path, and revisit once organizer demand is real.
  2. How long a template survives after an event. Twelve months makes re-enrolment unnecessary and is the better experience; per-event deletion is the stronger privacy position and a better answer to counsel. This is a positioning decision as much as a technical one.
  3. Whether resale-off events must offer a face-value returns queue. Recommendation: yes, mandatory. The support burden of the alternative will land on ReXell regardless of whose policy caused it.
  4. 1:N dedupe scope. Per-event is cheap and privacy-preserving; platform-wide catches serial farmers but concentrates biometric linkage across the whole user base. Legal review required before choosing.
  5. Gate hardware ownership. ReXell-owned kits give control of the field experience and a hardware line item; BYOD on venue tablets scales faster and controls less. Pilot both in P0.
  6. Chain selection and whether it is ever user-visible. If fans never see it, the choice is purely operational and reversible — which argues for not marketing the specific chain at all.
